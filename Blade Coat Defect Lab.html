Blade Coat Defect Lab

Slurry flow under the scraper, from a thin-film (lubrication) model. Move a slider and watch when the contact line, the web edge or the film surface goes wrong. Starting values are yours: 0.28 m/min, 1.90 mm machine height, 0.20 mm fibre, 10.5 Pa·s.

Inputs

What is measured and what is assumed
  • Yours: speed, machine height, fibre thickness, viscosity 10.5 Pa·s, design wet film 1.45 mm (the pressure default is back-calculated so the model returns 1.45 mm; it is not a measurement).
  • Assumed until you measure them: surface tension (0.07 N/m, water-like), contact angle on the blade, yield stress, shear-thinning index, bead pressure, land length, notch face length, density 1020 kg/m³.
  • Model limits: the film thickness uses lubrication theory with an effective viscosity. The contact-line height is the static gravity-plus-capillary meniscus (Young–Laplace), so dynamic effects at Ca ≈ 0.7 are not included. The web-edge and levelling results are order-of-magnitude indicators. Use them to see which lever matters, not to predict exact millimetres.
